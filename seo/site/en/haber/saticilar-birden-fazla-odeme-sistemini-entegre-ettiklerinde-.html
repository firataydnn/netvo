Merchants achieve better payment results when systems are strategically integrated

1 Eyl 2026

New research from PayPal Open and PYMNTS Intelligence reveals that merchants connecting multiple payment processors strategically see substantially improved payment performance.

A new study on merchant payment infrastructure integration highlights the growing importance of open payment systems in e-commerce operations. The August-September 2026 "Connecting What's Next: Open Infrastructure for the Future of Commerce" report, produced by PayPal Open and PYMNTS Intelligence, demonstrates that merchants successfully integrating multiple payment processors achieve significantly better payment performance outcomes. The research underscores a critical distinction: while adding payment processors is straightforward, generating real, measurable performance improvements from a networked system requires deeper strategic alignment and technical coordination.

This development reflects broader industry trends in payment technology. Over recent years, e-commerce platforms and payment service providers have invested heavily in API-based integrations and open payment networks, enabling merchants to coordinate diverse payment methods and processors within unified systems. However, the complexity lies not in adoption but in orchestration. Merchants now have access to multiple payment channels—credit cards, digital wallets, buy-now-pay-later options, and regional payment methods—yet leveraging these channels coherently remains a distinct challenge that demands both technical and operational expertise.

For e-commerce sellers and marketplace operators, this finding signals that payment strategy extends far beyond simple processor selection. Merely connecting additional payment systems yields minimal gains; true competitive advantage emerges when those systems work cohesively, with optimized data flow, reduced transaction failures, and customer behavior alignment. Merchants investing in proper integration and ongoing system optimization can reduce payment friction, lower decline rates, and improve conversion.

The emphasis on open payment architecture reflects a sector-wide shift away from proprietary, single-vendor solutions toward interoperable ecosystems. This transition offers merchants greater flexibility and competitive leverage, but simultaneously introduces operational complexity. Organizations must balance the benefits of payment diversification—reaching more customer segments, handling multiple currencies, accessing region-specific payment methods—with the operational burden of managing multiple processors, reconciling data, and ensuring consistent customer experiences across channels.

Looking forward, the research suggests that merchant payment success increasingly depends on platform choices and infrastructure decisions that prioritize integration efficiency and real-time visibility across payment flows.
