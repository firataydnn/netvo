In peak season, margin wins, not revenue: a 5-point checklist for sellers

October 27, 2026

Record holiday spending is expected, yet peak fulfillment fees and tighter inventory windows squeeze margins. Five items sellers should review this week.

Adobe expects 2026 year-end online sales to reach a record of roughly $275.1 billion, with demand already warming up in October. But record revenue does not mean sellers win: Amazon's US peak fulfillment fees, in force from 15 October 2026 to 14 January 2027, add an average of $0.32 per unit, and discounting pressure is high. So the question in peak season should not be "how much did I sell" but "how much is left per unit".

1) Recalculate unit economics. Reflect peak fees, shipping and returns per product. On low-margin items a $0.32 surcharge can erase profit; you may need to raise the price or trim ad spend.

2) Pull your inventory calendar forward. With tighter delivery and inbound windows, late stock costs both sales and ad investment. Prioritize fast movers and do not stack slow sellers in the warehouse during peak.

3) Cap discount depth. Matching every competitor price cut eats margin. Decide in advance the floor price for each product and judge campaigns by margin, not volume.

4) Keep delivery promises realistic. Large players such as Chewy build their last-mile edge on carrier partnerships and personalization. For a small seller the equivalent is giving a reliable date rather than promising speed you cannot keep.

5) Account for customs and tariff risk. Tariff refund lawsuits are ongoing in the US, and assuming fixed costs in a shifting rules environment is risky. Leave a small buffer in pricing.

In short, peak season looks like a volume race, but the winners will be those who protect margin within fee and inventory constraints. Completing this list this week, before the rush, is the cheapest safeguard.
