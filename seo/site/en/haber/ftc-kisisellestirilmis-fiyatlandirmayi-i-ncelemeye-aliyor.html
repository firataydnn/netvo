FTC Scrutinizes Personalized Pricing in E-Commerce

26 Ağu 2026

The U.S. Federal Trade Commission is investigating personalized pricing practices that show different prices to different customers.

The Federal Trade Commission (FTC) has launched a regulatory inquiry into personalized and surveillance-based pricing practices, requesting public comments on the matter. This action signals increased scrutiny of a widespread e-commerce and online retail practice: using customer data to display different prices to different shoppers. The FTC is specifically examining the legal and ethical boundaries of such strategies.

Personalized pricing has accelerated alongside advances in data analytics and artificial intelligence in recent years. Online marketplaces and e-commerce platforms can now deploy algorithms that use purchasing behavior, demographic information, location data, and browsing history to set individualized prices. This capability has raised concerns among consumers about fairness and transparency, prompting regulatory bodies to examine whether such practices violate consumer protection standards. Similar investigations are underway in several European jurisdictions.

For online sellers and marketplace operators, this development necessitates a comprehensive review of pricing algorithms and data governance policies. Retailers may need to exercise greater caution in deploying dynamic and personalized pricing strategies moving forward, and should begin preparing their operations for potential future compliance requirements and restrictions on how customer data is used in pricing decisions.
