AI assistants are narrowing shopping choices before consumers reach retailers

16 Eyl 2026

Shoppers are being directed to pre-filtered shortlists by artificial intelligence tools before they reach online retailers, creating a new competitive layer in e-commerce.

The way consumers shop is shifting rapidly. Instead of heading directly to major e-commerce platforms or stores, growing numbers of buyers are consulting AI assistants first. According to PYMNTS Intelligence's "The AI Power User Gets Pickier" report—the September edition of the Consumer AI Benchmark—this trend is gaining momentum. The data shows that 23% of consumers are now using AI tools to guide their purchasing decisions, and these systems typically narrow options down to just three or four choices before a purchase is finalized.

This development is reshaping the entire retail landscape, from e-commerce platforms to traditional retailers. AI tools—chatbots, recommendation engines, and AI-powered search—now sit between consumers and merchants as a filtering layer. The practical result: a retailer may not even get a chance to display their product to a potential customer until after an AI algorithm has decided whether to include them on the shortlist. This fundamentally redefines what "visibility" means in modern e-commerce.

For online sellers, the implications are stark. Being included in an AI assistant's shortlist is becoming as critical—if not more so—than traditional search engine optimization. Sellers must now optimize product listings, pricing, reviews, and metadata not just for Google or Amazon algorithms, but also for third-party AI recommendation systems. This demands greater sophistication in data management and marketing strategy.

From an industry perspective, three major shifts are underway. First, a new marketplace layer is emerging around AI-assisted shopping services (integrations with ChatGPT, Claude, and similar platforms). Second, retailers face a fresh competitive battleground: AI visibility. Third, established e-commerce platforms are accelerating their own AI initiatives, embedding proprietary assistants to keep the customer experience internalized and within their own ecosystems.

The report also highlights that AI power users—those who regularly rely on these tools—are becoming increasingly discerning. Early adopters may have blindly followed AI recommendations, but as experience accumulates, users develop critical judgment about these suggestions. For merchants, this means that simply appearing in AI results is no longer enough; they must deliver superior product data, customer service, and genuine value.

Looking ahead, AI-mediated shopping is likely to become standard rather than exceptional throughout 2024-2025. E-commerce businesses and sellers will need to embed AI optimization into the core of their strategy to remain competitive in this new environment.
