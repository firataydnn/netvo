FedEx brings customs duty certainty to Shopify merchants with new app

11 Eyl 2026

FedEx has integrated its Duty and Tax tool into Shopify, allowing sellers to display exact import costs at checkout and guarantee against unexpected tariff surcharges at delivery.

FedEx has launched an integration of its new Duty and Tax application on the Shopify platform, designed to address one of the persistent pain points in cross-border e-commerce: hidden tariff and customs charges. The tool calculates and guarantees import duties and taxes at the point of checkout, protecting both merchants and customers from surprise fees during customs clearance.

Unpredictable customs costs have long been a friction point in international selling. When buyers encounter unexpected tariff surcharges at delivery or during customs processing, transaction abandonment and customer disputes often follow. By embedding duty calculation directly into the Shopify checkout flow, FedEx removes that uncertainty and allows merchants to price products with confidence across borders.

For Shopify sellers, the practical benefit is clear: they can now combine shipping costs and customs obligations in a single, transparent calculation before the customer completes the purchase. This is particularly valuable for shipments to the European Union, United Kingdom, and other regions with complex tariff regimes. The integration reduces operational complexity and removes a key barrier to selling internationally.

This move reflects a broader industry shift. Logistics providers are increasingly embedding their services directly into e-commerce platforms, pulling traditionally opaque processes into the merchant's workflow. Amazon, UPS, and other carriers have either deployed similar solutions or are actively developing them.

For Shopify merchants—especially smaller brands exploring international expansion—this development expands market access. By making cross-border pricing predictable, FedEx's tool lowers the perceived risk of selling globally and removes a substantial operational hurdle. Merchants can now focus on product and marketing rather than navigating tariff tables and managing post-sale disputes over unexpected charges.
