Amazon's holiday peak fees take effect today: about $0.32 more per unit

October 15, 2026

Amazon's holiday peak fulfillment fees began on October 15; the real squeeze for sellers is the tighter inventory calendar.

Amazon's 2026 holiday peak fulfillment fees took effect on October 15, 2026 and run through January 14, 2027. They apply across Fulfillment by Amazon (FBA), Remote Fulfillment with FBA, Multi-Channel Fulfillment (MCF) and Buy with Prime. The per-unit increase over standard rates averages $0.32, with a 3.5% fuel and logistics surcharge added on top. Amazon said there are no new eligibility requirements and that deal fees — a $100 upfront fee per promotion plus a 1.5% variable fee, capped at $5,000 — stay the same as during Prime Day.

The real change is not the fees but the calendar for getting inventory into Amazon's warehouses. For Black Friday Week and Cyber Monday, the cutoffs tightened: Amazon Warehousing and Distribution (AWD) shipments must arrive by October 14, FBA shipments using minimal shipment splits by October 21, and FBA shipments using Amazon-optimized splits (the option most sellers choose) by October 28. Amazon is urging sellers to ship early, since warehouses focus on receiving holiday stock in September and October before shifting to order processing in November and December, which can tighten capacity. In Amazon's own forum, some sellers questioned why the 3.5% fuel surcharge is holding steady while oil prices have fallen.

Peak surcharges and the 3.5% add-on pull directly on per-unit profit for thin-margin products, so sellers need to revisit pricing and promotions at the SKU level. Inventory that arrives late risks both missing peak capacity and going out of stock during the highest-demand window. In short, this year the edge belongs to sellers who ship early and price in every line — commission, peak fee, surcharge, shipping — before the season starts.
