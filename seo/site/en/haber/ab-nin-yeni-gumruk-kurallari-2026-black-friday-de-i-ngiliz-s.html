EU customs rules put UK retailers' profitability to the test this Black Friday 2026

24 Ağu 2026

New EU import duties on low-value ecommerce goods will pressure UK retailers' margins for the first time during peak trading season, raising questions about sustainable profitability.

Black Friday 2026 marks the first major shopping peak under the European Union's tightened customs regulations for low-value ecommerce imports. UK retailers selling into European markets now face a fundamental challenge: protecting and growing profit margins while absorbing significantly higher customs and logistics costs. As Shopreturns CEO Paweł Zakielarz points out, the economics of peak trading season have shifted from a simple volume play to a margin-defense exercise.

The EU's regulatory tightening on import duties and VAT collection has accumulated pressure on cross-border online retailers over the past two years. For products with thin unit economics, customs clearance and tax obligations can now account for a substantial percentage of the total product cost. This regulatory shift is dismantling the traditional high-turnover, low-margin model that once dominated European e-commerce, forcing merchants to rethink pricing and supply-chain strategies in real time.

The coming Black Friday will serve as a live test of how UK and European sellers respond: whether they absorb costs through margin compression, pass them to consumers via price increases risking demand loss, or restructure fulfillment and sourcing entirely. Success will hinge on operational efficiency gains and strategic supply-chain redesigns that offset regulatory headwinds—making logistics and compliance infrastructure increasingly central to competitive advantage.
