Quince sells out first sample sale six hours ahead of schedule

2 Eyl 2026

Direct-to-consumer fashion brand Quince held its inaugural sample sale in New York, drawing a four-block-long queue and selling out all inventory six hours before the scheduled closing time.

Direct-to-consumer (D2C) fashion brand Quince experienced unexpectedly strong turnout at its first-ever sample sale event in New York. A four-block queue formed outside the venue with wait times exceeding two and a half hours, as hundreds of customers rushed to purchase discounted apparel including sweaters and other garments. The event sold out entirely six hours before its originally scheduled end time.

The sold-out event is now informing Quince's consideration of a larger, more permanent physical retail footprint. This exploration aligns with a broader trend among D2C fashion brands that began online but have increasingly tested physical pop-ups and limited-time experiences to drive customer engagement. The rapid sell-through suggests that brick-and-mortar retail still plays a strategic role in scaling digitally-native brands.

For Quince, the outcome provides valuable data on inventory velocity, customer demand, and geographic expansion potential. Physical sample sales allow D2C brands to gather direct customer feedback on product quality and design while testing whether new markets warrant permanent locations. The six-hour sell-out signals strong pent-up demand and offers management concrete metrics to inform decisions about expanding its offline presence in additional cities.
