Shein launches 737,000-square-foot automated distribution center in Indiana

14 Eyl 2026

Fast-fashion e-commerce platform Shein is activating its new automated distribution hub in Indiana this month, utilizing a goods-to-person fulfillment system to enhance efficiency and workplace safety.

Shein has begun operations at a 737,000-square-foot automated distribution center in Indiana, launching operations this month. The facility is equipped with a goods-to-person fulfillment system, a robotic-driven approach that brings products to workers rather than requiring personnel to navigate warehouse aisles. This automation method accelerates picking and packing workflows while reducing physical strain and safety risks for warehouse staff.

The Indiana facility represents a significant shift in how cross-border e-commerce companies approach logistics in North America. Traditionally reliant on direct shipments from Asia, ultra-fast fashion retailers are now establishing regional warehouses to cut delivery times and navigate U.S. customs delays. For players like Shein, domestic distribution infrastructure has become critical to competing on speed and customer satisfaction, particularly as supply chain pressures and last-mile delivery costs continue to rise.

The move signals strategic commitment to the U.S. market and carries broader implications for e-commerce operations. Automated distribution centers reduce long-term operational costs, shrink fulfillment times, and create competitive advantages in an increasingly crowded market. For sellers and platforms operating in the space, Shein's investment underscores the growing necessity of efficient logistics infrastructure to sustain growth and meet customer expectations in mature markets.
