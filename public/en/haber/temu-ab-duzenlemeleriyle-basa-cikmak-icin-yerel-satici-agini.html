Temu strengthens local seller network to navigate EU regulatory pressures

26 Ağu 2026

Facing heightened EU oversight, Temu is accelerating recruitment and support for local merchants to align its supply chain with tariff policy shifts and compliance requirements.

Chinese e-commerce platform Temu is recalibrating its supply chain strategy in response to intensifying regulatory scrutiny from the European Union. The company plans to significantly increase onboarding and support programs for domestic and regional sellers, as part of efforts to adapt to changing tariff and customs rules. By shifting toward a larger base of locally-sourced merchants, Temu aims to mitigate compliance risks while addressing policy concerns around rapid importation and pricing transparency.

This strategic pivot aligns with escalating EU enforcement actions against cross-border e-commerce platforms over the past months, driven by concerns about duty avoidance, product quality oversight, and market concentration. Competitors including Amazon and eBay have similarly prioritized local seller ecosystems as regulatory compliance has become central to platform viability in Europe. The shift signals that regulatory adaptation, not just growth, is now a competitive necessity for ultra-low-cost marketplaces.

For sellers, the development presents an opportunity to gain visibility on Temu's platform among EU consumers, but also requires readiness for evolving customs documentation, tariff cost absorption, and tax reporting obligations. The move suggests Temu intends to maintain significant presence in EU markets by structurally realigning its supply model—a long-term positioning rather than a temporary accommodation.
