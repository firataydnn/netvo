Asia-Pacific Cross-Border Real-Time Payments Accelerate via Regional Integration

2 Eyl 2026

Countries across Asia-Pacific are linking domestic instant payment systems across borders through initiatives like Project Nexus, enabling automated, low-cost international e-commerce transactions.

Asia-Pacific's payment infrastructure is undergoing fundamental transformation. Across the region, countries are connecting the domestic real-time payment systems they spent years building, stitching together a regional interoperability network. This shift enables commercial transactions to execute automatically, with minimal human intervention and faster settlement.

Project Nexus stands as the clearest example of this pivot. The initiative aims to integrate major economies' payment rails within the region. A seller in Thailand and a buyer in the Philippines can now execute transfers within minutes, at low cost, and through automated channels.

For e-commerce operators, this shift unlocks immediate advantages. As cross-border payments accelerate, transaction costs drop and processing times shrink. Alternative payment methods—particularly BNPL and regional digital wallets—gain traction on regional marketplaces. Today, most Asia-Pacific sellers face steep fees and settlement delays when moving money internationally.

Strategically, Asia-Pacific countries are establishing their own regional standard, partly as a counterweight to China's rapid digital payment expansion. Beyond financial independence, this infrastructure modernization turbocharges the region's e-commerce ecosystem. Within 18-24 months, the share of borderless transactions in regional commerce is likely to accelerate materially.
