Amazon launches standalone budget shopping app in emerging markets

7 Kas 2025

Amazon rolled out 'Bazaar', a separate mobile application focused on affordable products, across a dozen-plus developing nations in Asia, Africa, and Latin America.

Amazon has launched 'Bazaar' as a strategic response to intensifying competition in emerging market e-commerce, introducing a mobile-first platform designed specifically for price-sensitive consumers across Asia, Africa, and Latin America. Operating entirely separate from its core marketplace, Bazaar represents a significant shift in how the company approaches lower-income consumer segments where affordability and convenience are paramount. The app's deployment across more than a dozen developing nations signals Amazon's commitment to capturing market share in regions where alternative platforms like TikTok Shop and Shein have gained substantial traction through aggressive pricing and localized product offerings.

The competitive landscape in these markets has fundamentally shifted, with mobile-dependent consumers increasingly favoring platforms that prioritize low prices and rapid fulfillment over brand prestige. Bazaar's standalone structure allows Amazon to operate with leaner cost structures and testing frameworks that might otherwise be constrained by legacy infrastructure. This architectural decision reflects a pragmatic recognition that emerging market consumers have distinct shopping behaviors, requiring tailored inventory curation, payment options, and user interfaces that differ markedly from Amazon's premium-positioned main application.

For third-party sellers and merchant businesses, Bazaar opens new distribution channels in high-growth regions where volume and velocity increasingly outweigh margin expectations. Sellers can reach consumers actively seeking budget alternatives without competing against Amazon's premium product selection. As Amazon expands logistics infrastructure and payment networks across these regions, participating merchants may benefit from enhanced supply chain efficiency and access to previously underserved consumer bases, though they should expect price competition to remain fierce.
