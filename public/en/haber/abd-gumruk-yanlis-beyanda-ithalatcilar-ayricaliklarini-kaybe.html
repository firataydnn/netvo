U.S. Customs: Incorrect declarations could cost importers shipping rights

11 Eyl 2026

U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) will revoke import privileges for shippers with inaccurate information on file, effective September 18.

The U.S. Customs and Border Protection (CBP) is tightening its border procedures with a stricter enforcement policy. Beginning September 18, 2024, importers and shippers found to have inaccurate or incomplete customs information in their records will lose the right to import goods into the United States. This ruling affects all cross-border traders—from customs brokers to e-commerce sellers, D2C brands to large-scale exporters.

The policy reflects CBP's broader effort to strengthen border security and tax collection by enforcing data accuracy. Incorrect or incomplete customs declarations not only create logistics delays but also present risks of duty evasion. The shift underscores the growing importance of customs compliance, particularly as international trade has become more digital yet customs documentation remains largely manual. Errors in filing, outdated shipper information, and miscategorized goods are still common in global supply chains.

For sellers and exporters, this change requires a fundamental review of how customs operations are managed. Every detail in shipper records—sender identity, recipient details, product description, tariff classification, value declaration—must be verified and accurate. Failure to comply will effectively block access to the U.S. market, hitting online retailers sourcing from Asia, Europe, and elsewhere particularly hard. The decision is likely to increase demand for customs compliance software, automated documentation tools, and professional customs advisory services.
