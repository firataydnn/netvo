The Battle for AI-Driven Shopping: Platforms vs. Direct Merchants

6 Eyl 2026

As AI shopping tools proliferate, sellers and marketplaces are racing to determine whether customers will primarily use AI on external platforms or directly through merchant sites.

A strategic fork in e-commerce is emerging as sellers and platforms compete over where customers will engage with AI-powered shopping experiences. The core question—will shoppers rely on AI assistants hosted by external giants like Amazon, Google, and TikTok, or will they use AI tools built into independent merchant websites and apps?—has profound implications for sales channels, customer ownership, and competitive survival.

This tension arises as AI-driven product discovery tools rapidly gain adoption. Traditional search and marketplace interfaces are being displaced by conversational AI, personalized recommendations, and direct assistant interactions. For small and medium-sized sellers, this shift poses an existential challenge: where will their customers look first, and who controls that entry point?

Marketplace operators—Amazon, eBay, Shopify—are embedding AI capabilities directly into their platforms, reinforcing their role as customer gatekeepers and justifying their commission structures. Large DTC (direct-to-consumer) brands are simultaneously investing heavily in proprietary AI chatbots and recommendation engines. The result is a fragmented landscape where sellers must navigate multiple channels simultaneously, each with different economics and strategic implications.

For merchants, the stakes are clear: sales acquired via external platforms carry higher fees and less customer data ownership, while direct sales preserve margins and first-party data but require costlier customer acquisition. AI amplifies both paths—making platform discovery more efficient and enabling richer on-site personalization—but also raises the bar on technical capability and investment. Sellers who fail to implement AI-powered tools risk becoming invisible in a customer journey increasingly mediated by machine learning.
