Three invisible costs waiting for sellers as Q4 2026 begins

9 Eki 2026

Peak season explodes revenue, but three costs quietly eat the profit: logistics surcharges, ad competition, and the returns wave. Price each one per unit.

The final quarter is e-commerce's highest-revenue stretch — and the one that leaks the most profit. First, logistics: platforms like Amazon layer peak-season fees on top of a permanent fuel surcharge, pushing per-unit cost to its yearly high. Second, advertising: through Black Friday and Christmas, cost-per-click can double, so you spend more ad money to make the same sale. Third, returns: the return rate on holiday gifts runs well above the yearly average, and a return hits twice — shipping plus reprocessing.

The fix isn't 'sell more,' it's 'enter with net-per-unit math.' For every SKU, add up four lines: platform commission + logistics/surcharge + estimated ad share + estimated return cost. If that total doesn't leave net profit under your selling price, pushing that product in peak season only inflates revenue while closing the year at a loss. The Q4 winner isn't the biggest seller — it's the one who knows exactly what's left in the pocket per unit.
