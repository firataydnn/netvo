Shein Launches Automated Distribution Hub in Indiana

14 Eyl 2026

Shein is opening a 737,000-square-foot automated distribution center in Indiana this month, equipped with goods-to-person fulfillment technology to accelerate order processing and warehouse safety.

Shein is bringing online a new automated distribution center in Indiana this month, spanning 737,000 square feet. The facility's core is a goods-to-person (G2P) fulfillment system, a technology that brings inventory to workers rather than requiring workers to hunt for items across the warehouse floor. This approach reduces picking time, minimizes errors, and improves employee safety by limiting repetitive motion and travel within the facility.

The Indiana investment underscores Shein's strategy to deepen its logistics footprint in North America, moving beyond pure marketplace model reliance. As a fast-fashion e-commerce platform, Shein has built its brand on ultra-low price points; the next competitive frontier is delivery speed and operational efficiency. Automating warehouses cuts labor costs per unit processed and accelerates shipment cycles—capabilities that Amazon, Walmart, and other mega-retailers have wielded for years. By owning and operating its own distribution infrastructure, Shein can tighten control over its supply chain and reduce dependency on third-party fulfillment partners.

For Shein sellers and marketplace participants, the implications are mixed. Faster order-to-doorstep timelines can raise the bar for customer satisfaction across all vendors on the platform. However, as Shein tightens its own fulfillment operations, seller autonomy and margin dynamics may shift. On the broader regulatory front, the U.S. continues debating tariffs and de-minimis exceptions that have favored Shein's model; vertical integration of logistics could allow the company to absorb margin pressure while maintaining competitive pricing—a strategic hedge in an evolving regulatory climate.
